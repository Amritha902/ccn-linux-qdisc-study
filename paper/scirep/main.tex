\title{Runtime parameter adaptation for a flow-queueing active queue
discipline: a controlled evaluation on stock Linux}

\author[1]{Amritha S}
\author[1]{Yugeshwaran P}
\author[1]{Deepti Annuncia}
\affil[1]{Vellore Institute of Technology, School of Electronics Engineering,
Department of Electronics and Communication Engineering, Chennai, India}

\keywords{active queue management, bufferbloat, network latency, Linux traffic
control, eBPF, reproducibility}

\begin{abstract}
The default Linux queue discipline, \texttt{fq\_codel}, exposes four parameters
fixed at configuration time. Runtime adaptation of such parameters has been
proposed repeatedly, but never evaluated under conditions that separate a
controller's computational cost from the effect of its decisions. We implement
a controller that adapts all four parameters using an additive-increase
multiplicative-decrease policy informed by extended Berkeley Packet Filter flow
telemetry, and evaluate it on an unmodified kernel against eight alternative
queue disciplines under an identical bottleneck, with three repetitions per
condition and a sham-controller condition that applies no change. Flow queueing
with a delay target reduces 95th-percentile latency by 99.0 per cent relative to
an unmanaged queue at indistinguishable goodput. Adapting that discipline's
parameters reduces mean queue occupancy by 9.6 per cent under a steady load and
produces no improvement under a load whose flow count varies, while an existing
queue discipline requiring no controller outperforms the adapted system by a
larger margin than the adaptation achieves. We also show that latency measured
by a sparse probe and by the bulk flows can differ by an order of magnitude and
can invert the ranking of two disciplines, and that goodput computed from bytes
sent rather than received exceeds link capacity.
\end{abstract}
