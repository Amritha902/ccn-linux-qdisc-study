\title{\vspace{-1cm}When does adapting an active queue discipline's parameters
pay? A scaling law in \texttt{target}/RTT, measured on stock Linux}
\author{Amritha S \and Yugeshwaran P \and Deepti Annuncia}
\date{Vellore Institute of Technology, Chennai}
\begin{document}
\twocolumn[\maketitle
\begin{abstract}\noindent
The default Linux queue discipline, \texttt{fq\_codel}, exposes four parameters
fixed at configuration time. Whether adapting them at runtime is worth doing
has been answered both ways, with systems reporting gains and evaluations
reporting none. We show that both answers are right, and that which one applies
is decided by a single dimensionless number. Across 178 runs spanning three
workloads, eight alternative queue disciplines and path round-trip times from
2 to 200\,ms, the benefit of adaptation is governed not by RTT but by the ratio
$r = \texttt{target}/\text{RTT}$, saturating at \LawCeiling\ per cent with half
the benefit at $r = \LawHalfBenefit$. The hypothesis, its three predictions and
their numerical thresholds were committed before the campaign that tests them
ran. Ratio invariance, the prediction able to refute the claim, holds to 2.0
percentage points across a sixteenfold range of RTT; held-out ratios are
predicted to 1.4 points; and at a fixed 20\,ms path the benefit moves from
1.4 per cent to 17.6 per cent on changing $r$ alone, which no account in terms
of RTT reproduces. The crossover is not specific to CoDel: fitted
independently, plain \texttt{codel} and PIE, which shares no mechanism with
CoDel beyond having a delay target, place it within a factor of two. Published
guidance places a correctly configured deployment at $r$ of 0.05 to 0.10, so
half the available benefit arrives five to ten times outside that envelope:
adaptation is a remedy for misconfiguration rather than an improvement on
correct configuration, which unifies the null results in this work and in the
literature as one effect. Flow queueing with a delay target remains the
dominant effect, reducing 95th-percentile latency by 99.0 per cent relative to
an unmanaged queue at indistinguishable goodput, and an existing discipline
requiring no controller outperforms the adapted system.
\end{abstract}
